# Teaching and visual standards

## Learner model

Assume a capable learner with some programming familiarity but no reliable foundation in vectors, matrices, algebra, trigonometry, coordinate frames, mechanics, control, or electronics theory.

Introduce symbols only after the physical picture. Define every symbol, unit, frame, input, and output. Offer several optional bridges—such as interfaces, typed contracts, event streams, data analysis, testing, electronics, fabrication, or repair—without assuming that any learner has one specific background or pretending software simulation proves physical success.

## Lesson order

Teach in this order when applicable:

1. physical picture and everyday analogy;
2. vocabulary and system boundary;
3. visual model;
4. symbols and units;
5. one worked numeric example with no skipped steps;
6. physical or browser-based exercise;
7. failure case and safety limit;
8. teach-back or diagnostic check;
9. authoritative sources.

Do not force textbook chapter order when a prerequisite bridge is needed. Do not use a concept before the learner has met its prerequisites.

## Content integrity

- Keep explanations original and cite authoritative source pages.
- Prefer official documentation, primary textbooks' public companion material, and publisher pages.
- Do not reproduce chapters, transcripts, proprietary figures, or hosted videos.
- Distinguish source facts, Robot/Shift synthesis, inference, and job-market evidence.
- Date job-market evidence and retain traceable postings.

## Visual integrity

- Prefer deterministic SVG and charts over generated raster imagery.
- Derive geometry and labels from validated data.
- State units and coordinate frames on the visual or immediately beside it.
- Include meaningful failure states: wrong frame, wrong sign, singularity, limit, saturation, collision, stale data, or unsafe command.
- Ensure visible labels, accessible names, keyboard controls, and light/dark contrast.
- Never add decoration that can be mistaken for a measurement, trajectory, sensor, joint, or connection.

## Tutor integrity

- Ground responses in the displayed lesson and approved sources.
- Explain simply but do not invent facts, commands, equations, or citations.
- Use GitHub-Flavored Markdown and KaTeX-compatible LaTeX.
- Never return raw HTML.
- Never direct generated commands to powered hardware without limits, emergency stop, and human review.
